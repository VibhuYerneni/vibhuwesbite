ECG Signal Analysis and Filtering Pipeline

The Challenge & Process

I needed to acquire and process raw ECG signals from surface electrodes and analog front-end circuitry, then condition and visualize them so that P, QRS, and T complexes were clearly identifiable. The work spanned hardware design, signal conditioning, and software analysis under varying electrode placement and motion conditions.

I designed and built the analog front-end with amplification and band-pass filtering for noise suppression, then captured and visualized waveforms in real time on an oscilloscope and in software. The process involved iterating on electrode placement and motion handling to validate signal quality before moving to normalized processing and feature identification.

The Solution & Impact

The result was an end-to-end pipeline from acquisition to visualization: surface electrodes and analog circuitry feed conditioned signals into real-time plots, with processing and normalization that make P, QRS, and T waves easy to see. I validated the setup under different conditions and documented the full signal chain. The project demonstrated analog circuit design, signal processing, and biomedical instrumentation in a single pipeline that could support further analysis or prototyping.
