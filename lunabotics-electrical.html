SEDS Lunabotics - Electrical

The Challenge & Process

The Lunabotics rover required reliable I²C connectivity across multiple boards and accurate position tracking on the competition field. I took on the electrical design for two boards: an I²C power and communication hub to connect the Raspberry Pi to downstream hardware with address isolation, and a UWB beacon board for triangulation so the robot could be located in real time.

I designed both boards in KiCad from schematic through PCB layout, with configurable pull-ups and reset handling for bus reliability, jumper-selectable 3.3V/5V power where needed, and a LiPo charging circuit on the beacon board so the field system could run without disposable batteries. The process involved iterating with the software team so the hardware would plug into the ROS2-driven control stack without bottlenecks.

The Solution & Impact

The I²C hub uses a Raspberry Pi and TCA9548A multiplexer to give multiple downstream boards clean address isolation and improved bus reliability. Full schematic and PCB were done in KiCad, enabling seamless hardware–software integration with the team’s ROS2 drivers and control nodes. The beacon board hosts three external UWB beacons that communicate with four on-robot beacons for triangulation and position tracking on the field; it includes microcontroller-based control and a rechargeable LiPo with integrated charging.

Both boards were brought up, debugged, and validated in system-level testing. They supported the rover’s 3rd place finish at the NASA Lunabotics competition and are documented for future rover iterations.
